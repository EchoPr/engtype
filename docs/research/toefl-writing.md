# TOEFL iBT Writing: official assessment standards (as of 2026-10-07)

Research for engtype's AI essay scoring. Only ETS sources are used as evidence. Tags used below:

- **[verbatim]**: a short quote from the source. The rubrics are ETS copyright, so quotes are kept to short phrases.
- **[paraphrase]**: our restatement of the source.
- **UNVERIFIED**: no primary source was found.
- **CONFLICT**: two ETS sources disagree.
- **(derived)**: our own interpretation, not an ETS statement.

Page references: "PDF p." means the page number in the PDF file, and "printed p." means the page number printed on the page.

## Summary

- **Today's test (since 2026-01-21):** the Writing section has 3 task types and 12 items, with an estimated time of 23 minutes. There are 10 Build a Sentence items, 1 Write an Email (7 minutes) and 1 Write for an Academic Discussion (10 minutes; "an effective response will contain at least 100 words"). [S1 PDF p.4, 13–15; S3 PDF p.6; S2 PDF p.30]
- **Only 2 tasks are rated by rubric.** Write an Email and Academic Discussion are each scored 0–5 with their own rubric. Build a Sentence is scored 0 or 1 against a key. The section raw score runs 0–20. It is converted to a 1–6 band, in 0.5 steps, by weighted equipercentile linking, not by a linear formula. [S2 PDF p.30, 34–35; S4]
- **AI does most of the scoring, with humans checking it.** Writing responses go to "ETS proprietary AI scoring engines as well as human scoring". Humans score responses where the engine "lacks confidence", and they rate a random sample for quality control. The engine's features are: number of sentences, discourse coherence, similarity to the prompt, sentence variety, word frequency, collocations, politeness markers, and grammar/usage/mechanics errors. [S2 PDF p.35–37, 39]
- **The CEFR mapping is the same in every section.** 6 = C2, 5–5.5 = C1, 4–4.5 = B2, 3–3.5 = B1, 2–2.5 = A2, 1–1.5 = A1. On the old 0–30 scale the Writing minimums were B1 13, B2 17 and C1 24. A new concordance maps old Writing 29–30 to band 6. [S1 PDF p.21–22; S4 p.14; S10]
- **The 2023 change:** on 2023-07-26 ETS replaced Independent Writing (30 min, about 300 words) with Writing for an Academic Discussion (10 min, 100+ words). From 2023 to 2025 the section had 2 tasks: Integrated (20 min, 150–225 words) and Academic Discussion. [S7; S8 printed p.10–11; S9]
- **The 2026 change:** Integrated Writing is no longer in the test. ETS states that the Academic Discussion task "remained unchanged" in 2026. Its rubric text has stayed the same since 2023, apart from one word. [S4 p.2; S5 vs S6 vs S9-rubric]
- **Our app's "toefl-independent" format is obsolete.** The task has not appeared on the TOEFL iBT since 2023-07-26. The format our app is missing is **Write an Email**.
- **Memorised answers and templates:** ETS calls memorised answers a policy violation and uses similarity-detection software. Copying the prompt or the stimulus is penalised in the rubric: a fully copied response scores 0, and "mostly borrowed" language is a level-1 feature. [S11; S12; S5]
- **Length:** the only official word guidance for a current task is "at least 100 words" for Academic Discussion. Write an Email has no word count, only "Write as much as you can and in complete sentences". The Academic Discussion sample responses that ETS scored 4 and 5 are roughly 110–175 words. [S1 PDF p.14–17; S9 p.12–13]

| Task | Items | Time | Prompt structure | Length guidance | Raw points |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Build a Sentence | 10 | (part of the 23-min section) | One intact sentence, then a reply made of jumbled words or phrases. The test taker rebuilds a grammatical reply "that is an appropriate reply in the context". | n/a | 0/1 each, 0–10 total |
| Write an Email | 1 | **7 min** | A short scenario (academic or social), sometimes with graphics. | Write for an Academic Discussion | 1 | **10 min** | The course subject. A professor's post that frames the topic and ends with an opinion question. Two student posts that take different positions. Sources: S1 PDF p.4, 13–16 [verbatim quotes as marked]; S2 PDF p.22–24, 30 (Table 2); S3 PDF p.2, 6, 15. The section total is 12 items, 0–20 raw points, about 23 min (S1 p.4; S2 Table 2; S3 p.2).

What each rated task measures, according to ETS [paraphrase of S1 p.14–15 and S2 PDF p.23–24]:

- **Email**: a multi-sentence text that (1) "achieves the designated communicative goal, following basic social conventions", (2) is adequately elaborated, clear and cohesive, (3) uses a range of grammar and vocabulary accurately and appropriately, and (4) follows the mechanical conventions (spelling, punctuation, capitalisation).
- **Academic Discussion**: a multi-sentence text that (1) "clearly elaborates an argument for a position, responding to arguments, and/or using information provided in short texts", (2) is adequately supported, clear and cohesive, and (3)–(4) are the same as for Email.

CEFR targets in the blueprint [verbatim, S3 PDF p.6]: Build a Sentence A1–C2; Write an Email B1–C2; Academic Discussion B1–C2.

The 2026 report says the Academic Discussion task "remained unchanged" in the 2026 update [verbatim, S4 p.2].

## Q2. Rubrics and how scores are produced

### Shared structure of both current rubrics (S5, © 2025)

- Each rubric has 6 levels, 0–5. Each of levels 5 to 1 has a label, a holistic summary sentence, and "A typical response displays the following" bullets. ETS calls these "typical" features: the rubric is **holistic** and the bullets are not separate sub-scores.
- The **level labels** are the same in both rubrics [verbatim]: 5 "A fully successful response", 4 "A generally successful response", 3 "A partially successful response", 2 "A mostly unsuccessful response", 1 "An unsuccessful response".
- The **score-0 rule** is the same in both rubrics [verbatim]: "The response is blank, rejects the topic, is not in English, is entirely copied from the prompt, is entirely unconnected to the prompt or consists of arbitrary keystrokes."

### Write an Email rubric (S5 p.1). Dimensions: elaboration of the purpose, syntax/vocabulary, social conventions, errors.

| Score | Paraphrase | Short verbatim anchors |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | Effective and clear, with consistent control of the language. Elaboration fully supports the purpose. Sentence structures vary effectively, and word choice is precise and idiomatic. Social conventions are appropriate throughout. Almost no errors beyond normal timed-writing slips. | "consistent facility"; "precise, idiomatic word choice"; "Consistent use of appropriate social conventions (e.g., politeness, register, organization of information and formulation of actions such as requests, refusals, criticisms, etc.)"; errors limited to "common typos or common misspellings or substitutions like there/their" |
| 4 | Mostly effective and easy to understand. Elaboration is adequate. There is syntactic variety and appropriate word choice. Social conventions are mostly appropriate. Few errors. | "mostly effective and easily understood"; "Adequate elaboration"; "Mostly appropriate social conventions"; "Few lexical or grammatical errors" |
| 3 | The task is generally done, but limited language makes parts of the message unclear. Elaboration only partly supports the purpose. Syntax and vocabulary range is moderate. Some noticeable errors in structure, word forms, idiom or social conventions. | "generally accomplishes the task"; "partially supports the communicative purpose"; "moderate range of syntax and vocabulary"; "Some noticeable errors" |
| 2 | An attempt that is mostly ineffective. The message may be limited or hard to interpret. Elaboration is limited or irrelevant. There is some connected sentence-level language with limited range. Errors accumulate. | "mostly ineffective"; "Limited or irrelevant elaboration"; "An accumulation of errors" |
| 1 | An ineffective attempt that may be close to unintelligible. Almost no elaboration. Telegraphic language. Serious, frequent errors. Little original language: most of the coherent language is borrowed from the stimulus. | "Telegraphic language (i.e., short and/or disconnected phrases and sentences)"; "Minimal original language; any coherent language is mostly borrowed from the stimulus" |
| 0 | See the shared score-0 rule. | n/a |

Note: levels 2 and 1 of the Email rubric say "exhibits **one or more** of the following". Any single bullet can therefore put a response at that level. Levels 5–3 say "displays the following" [verbatim, S5 p.1].

Official calibration example [S1 PDF p.15]: a sample Email response of about 115 words scored **4**. ETS's comment: verb tense issues ("…if my poems can successfully sent") "are consequential enough to prevent this response from earning a score of 5" [verbatim].

Versions of this rubric: S9-rubric (2023), S6 (2024) and S5 (2025) have the same content. The only differences are punctuation and one wording change at level 1 ("may display" in 2024, "displays" in 2025). We checked this with a word-level diff of the three PDFs.

Official calibration examples:

- S9 PDF p.12–13, 2023 practice set:
  - A response of about 170 words scored **5**. It does not mention the other students' posts. Its minor errors were judged "fairly minor and such errors might be expected when writing under timed conditions" [verbatim].
  - A response of about 110 words scored **4** because "the number and type of errors in grammar and vocabulary prevent it from reaching the highest score level" [verbatim]. The ETS comment also says the argument "might have been a little stronger" with broader support.
- S1 PDF p.16–17: a response of about 120 words that engages with one student ("While I get Andrew's point…") scored **5**. It had one missing article.

### Retired rubrics (for reference)

- **Integrated Writing** (S6 p.1, © 2024; levels 0–5). The rubric is built around selecting the important lecture points and connecting them accurately to the reading. Level 3 may "omit one major key point made in the lecture" [verbatim]. Level 0 "merely copies sentences from the reading" [verbatim].
- **Independent Writing** (S8, Appendix, printed p.58 / PDF p.65; levels 0–5). The criteria are addressing the topic and task, organisation and development, "unity, progression and coherence" [verbatim], and language facility. Level 0 "merely copies words from the topic, rejects the topic, or is otherwise not connected to the topic" [verbatim].

### From task scores to the reported Writing score

1. **Raw scores** [paraphrase, S2 PDF p.30 Table 2, p.34–35]: Build a Sentence earns 1/0 per item (0–10). Email earns 0–5 and Academic Discussion earns 0–5. **Section raw total: 0–20.**
2. **Scoring the rated tasks** [verbatim, S2 PDF p.35]: responses "are evaluated using ETS proprietary AI scoring engines as well as human scoring to enhance accuracy and consistency of scores."
   - [paraphrase, S2 PDF p.39] The engines are trained on human ratings and monitored in real time. "For responses where the automated scoring lacks confidence or encounters difficulty, human raters step in to provide scores" [verbatim]. Certified human raters regularly review a random sample.
   - [verbatim, S2 PDF p.40] "Responses from each test taker are scored by more than one rater."
   - Human–machine correlation for Writing is 0.86, and human–human is 0.85 (N = 1,914) [S2 PDF p.39 Table 7].
   - The exact way human and AI scores are combined for one response (contributory average vs. AI-only with human backup) is **UNVERIFIED**; see the CONFLICT in Open questions.
3. **Features the AI engine uses** [verbatim terms, S2 PDF p.36–37 Tables 3–4]:
   - Content: "Number of sentences", "Discourse coherence", "Similarity to question prompt".
   - Variety: "Sentence variety", "Word frequency", "Correctness of collocations".
   - Social conventions (Email only): "Use of politeness indicators (e.g., modals, hedge words)".
   - Accuracy (listed for Email): grammaticality, grammatical errors, word or usage errors, mechanical errors.
   - Table 4 (Academic Discussion) in the PDF shows only the Content and Variety rows. The running text on PDF p.36 lists accuracy features for "the Writing section" in general.
   - The 2026 documents do not use the name "e-rater". The e-rater page (S18) still describes the retired Independent and Integrated tasks.
4. **Conversion** [verbatim, S2 PDF p.35]: "The total writing and speaking raw score points are converted to a scaled score through innovative weighted equipercentile linking procedures that account for minor variations in difficulty among the different test versions." The reported scores "are not equal to the number or percentage of raw score points earned nor a simple common linear transformation of them" [verbatim, S2 PDF p.34]. **The raw-to-band table is not published** (UNVERIFIED).
5. **Reported scale** [paraphrase, S1 PDF p.21; S13]: Writing is reported as a 1–6 band in 0.5 steps. The Overall score is the average of the four section bands, rounded to the nearest half band (S13 gives the example "5.25 → 5.5"). Writing reliability is 0.87 and SEM is 0.36 band [S2 PDF p.48 Table 11].
6. **Before 2026** [paraphrase, S8 PDF p.28–30]: the Integrated and Independent tasks were each scored 0–5 by one human plus a machine score, averaged. The section was reported 0–30. RM-23-06 says the same "contributory scoring approach" (human plus machine) "will be used in the updated TOEFL iBT test" [verbatim, S8 PDF p.30]. That sentence refers to the 2023 update.

## Q4. TOEFL and the CEFR

### Current 1–6 scale (the same in every section and Overall)

| CEFR | Writing band | Source |
|---|---|---|
| C2 | 6 | S1 PDF p.21 Table 2; S2 PDF p.42 Table 9; S4 p.14 Table 7; S14 |
| C1 | 5–5.5 | same |
| B2 | 4–4.5 | same |
| B1 | 3–3.5 | same |
| A2 | 2–2.5 | same |
| A1 | 1–1.5 | same |

**Method for Writing** [paraphrase, S2 PDF p.41–42; S4 p.11–13]:

1. The tasks and rubrics were compared with CEFR descriptors.
2. An ETS-internal standard-setting study used the performance-profile method on portfolios of each test taker's written responses, to set the minimum raw score for each CEFR level.
3. Equipercentile linking was used to carry those minimums over to the 2026 base forms (about 500 Writing test takers).

ETS also publishes CEFR-based Writing performance descriptors for each band [S4 Appendix A; S3 PDF p.15]. Examples:

- B2: produces well-organised, coherent text with a range of linking devices; uses complex structures "rigidly with some inaccuracy" [verbatim].
- B1: "major errors still occur when expressing more complex thoughts" [verbatim].

## Q5. Official guidance on length, off-topic, memorised and copied responses

| Topic | Guidance | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Length (Academic Discussion) | "An effective response will contain at least 100 words." [verbatim] Also: "There's no maximum length for your response, but a good response is usually at least 100 words." [verbatim] | S1 PDF p.15; S9 PDF p.4, 11; S12 |
| Length (Email) | No count given. "Write as much as you can and in complete sentences." [verbatim] | S1 PDF p.14 |
| Length in the scoring engine | "Number of sentences" is a Content feature. | S2 PDF p.37 |
| Off-topic | Score 0 if the response "rejects the topic" or "is entirely unconnected to the prompt" [verbatim]. A partly relevant response is described at level 2 ("only partially relevant") and level 3 ("mostly relevant"). | S5 |
| Copying the prompt or stimulus | Score 0 if "entirely copied from the prompt" [verbatim]. Level 1: "any coherent language is mostly borrowed from the stimulus" [verbatim]. The engine uses "Similarity to question prompt". For Academic Discussion: "Don't just copy what the other students have written" [verbatim]; you "may refer to one of the other student posts to agree or disagree, but make sure to use your own words" [verbatim]; "add your own perspective … not merely repeat ideas that have already been stated" [verbatim]. | S5; S2 PDF p.37; S12; S9 PDF p.5 |
| Memorised and template responses | "Using memorized answers is a violation of testing policies." [verbatim] ETS uses "detection software to identify writing passages that are similar to outside sources or other test takers' responses" [verbatim]. On templates: "you must be using your own words" [verbatim], and "If you use a template, there is a high probability of providing responses similar to those of other test-takers" [verbatim]. ## Derived scoring guide for AI (derived)

> **This section is OUR INTERPRETATION** of the sources above. It is not ETS material, and ETS has not validated it. References in brackets point to the sourced items above. Use this guide for formative feedback only. Present any score as an "estimated TOEFL-style 0–5". Do not present it as a predicted official band, because the raw-to-band conversion is not published (Q2, step 4).

### General principles (derived)

- G1. Score **holistically**. Pick the level whose summary sentence fits best, and use the bullets as evidence. Do not add up separate sub-scores [Q2: shared structure, "typical response"].
- G2. **Errors are judged by their effect on meaning, not by count alone.** At level 5, timed-writing slips (typos, there/their) are allowed. "Few" errors means level 4, "some noticeable" means 3, "accumulation" means 2, and "serious and frequent" means 1 [S5]. The official 4-vs-5 decisions both turned on errors that were "consequential" or "distracting" [S1 p.15; S9 p.13].
- G3. **Language borrowed from the prompt or stimulus does not count as the writer's own production** [S5 levels 1 and 0; S2 "Similarity to question prompt"].
- G4. Reward clear development within the time limit. Do not demand a polished essay structure [S16; S8 p.19 on the "five paragraph essay" washback].

### Task A: Write an Email (0–5) [S5 p.1; S1 p.14–15; S2 Table 3]

**(a) Observable features per level**

| Level | Check for |
|---|---|
| 5 | All 3 required bullet points are covered and elaborated (reasons, details, specifics). Greeting, purpose, sign-off and register fit the recipient. Requests and complaints are phrased politely (modals, hedges). Varied sentences (subordinate clauses, conditionals). Precise, idiomatic wording. At most 1–2 trivial slips. |
| 4 | All 3 points are covered, though some are thinly developed. Conventions are mostly right (for example one register slip). Some variety. A few errors that do not block meaning. |
| 3 | The task is generally done, but at least one point is underdeveloped, or the reader has to work to understand part of it. A moderate range. Noticeable errors in structure, word form or conventions (for example an abrupt or too casual request). |
| 2 | An attempt is visible, but required points are missing or irrelevant. Short connected sentences with a limited range. Errors accumulate and blur the message. |
| 1 | Telegraphic fragments, almost no elaboration, and/or mostly words copied from the scenario. |
| 0 | Blank, not English, entirely copied, unrelated to the scenario, or keystrokes. |

**(b) What separates adjacent levels**

- **5 vs 4:** precise, idiomatic wording and *consistent* conventions versus merely appropriate ones. Also whether any error is consequential: one verb-form error that affects meaning was enough to cap the official sample at 4 [S1 p.15].
- **4 vs 3:** whether elaboration is *adequate* for every required point or only *partial*. Whether errors are "few" or "noticeable". Whether the message is fully clear or only "generally" accomplished.
- **3 vs 2:** whether the communicative purpose is still achieved (3) or the message is "limited or difficult to interpret" (2). Whether elaboration is partial or "limited or irrelevant".
- **2 vs 1:** whether there is connected sentence-level text (2) or telegraphic fragments and borrowed language (1).
- **1 vs 0:** whether there is any original attempt at the task, or the response is blank, copied, or off-topic.

**(c) Rules split by type**

| Deterministic (code) | Judgement (LLM) |
|---|---|
| Empty or whitespace only → 0 [S5] | Whether each of the 3 required points is *addressed* and *elaborated* |
| Mostly non-English characters or a non-English language → 0 [S5] | Appropriateness of register and politeness to the recipient [S5 "social conventions"] |
| Keystroke noise (low dictionary-word ratio, repeated characters) → 0 [S5] | Precision and idiomaticity of word choice |
| Near-total n-gram overlap with the prompt text → 0. High overlap → flag a level-1 ceiling [S5; S2] | Whether errors are trivial, noticeable, or meaning-blocking |
| Counts: words, sentences, average sentence length, distinct sentence openings. Use as *evidence* only, never as hard cut-offs [S2 Table 3]. No official minimum length exists. | Whether short sentences are "telegraphic" (level 1) or connected (level 2+) |
| Greeting and sign-off present (feeds conventions; not decisive alone) | Overall holistic level |

### Task B: Write for an Academic Discussion (0–5) [S5 p.2; S1 p.15–17; S9 p.4–5, 12–13; S12]

**(a) Observable features per level**

| Level | Check for |
|---|---|
| 5 | A clear position that answers the professor's question. At least one reason developed with explanation and a specific example or detail. Adds something beyond the two student posts. Very clear throughout. Effective variety of structures. Precise, idiomatic vocabulary. Only timed-writing slips. |
| 4 | Relevant position and adequate support. Ideas are easy to follow. Some variety. A few errors that may be noticeable but do not obscure meaning. |
| 3 | Mostly relevant. Part of the support is missing, vague or off-point (for example an example that does not connect back to the claim). Some variety. Noticeable errors in sentence structure, word form or idiom. |
| 2 | An attempt to take part, but ideas are poorly developed or only partly relevant, or hard to follow. Limited range. Errors accumulate. |
| 1 | A few words and phrases, few or no coherent ideas, mostly repeating the prompt or the posts. |
| 0 | Blank, not English, entirely copied, unrelated, rejects the topic, or keystrokes. |

**(b) What separates adjacent levels**

- **5 vs 4:** whether elaboration is "well-elaborated" or only "adequately elaborated". Whether syntax and lexis are precise and idiomatic or just appropriate. How many errors there are and how distracting they are (the official 4 had "multiple small errors" that were "distracting" [S9 p.13]).
- **4 vs 3:** whether every part of the support is relevant and clear, or part of an explanation or example is "missing, unclear or irrelevant". Whether errors are "few" or "some noticeable".
- **3 vs 2:** whether ideas are mostly understandable, or "hard to follow" and "poorly elaborated or only partially relevant". Whether there is some variety or a limited range.
- **2 vs 1:** whether there is at least one coherent idea, or only words and phrases with language borrowed from the stimulus.
- **Not a discriminator:** naming or rebutting the other students is *optional*. The official 2023 sample scored 5 without mentioning them [S9 p.12]. Merely restating their points, however, does not count as a contribution [S9 p.5; S12].

**(c) Rules split by type**

| Deterministic (code) | Judgement (LLM) |
|---|---|
| The 0-rules, as for Email [S5] | Is a position on the professor's question stated? Is it relevant? |
| Overlap with the professor's or students' posts: near-total → 0; high → level-1/2 ceiling flag [S5; S12] | Is the support the writer's own, or a paraphrase of the student posts? [S9 p.5; S12] |
| Word count < 100 → *advisory* flag ("below ETS's 'effective response' guidance") [S1 p.15]. Never an automatic cap: no official rule converts length into a score. | Depth of elaboration: explanation, example, detail, and whether they link back to the claim |
| Sentence count and variety metrics as evidence [S2 Table 4] | Clarity, cohesion, academic-but-forum register [S9 p.5] |
| Template detection: matching against a stored list of common TOEFL template phrases or other users' submissions → flag and explain the policy [S11]. Do not cut the score automatically. | Whether "template" sentences carry real content or are filler |

### Legacy: TOEFL Independent essay (only if kept as a legacy format)

Score it with the Independent rubric in S8 Appendix (PDF p.65). Its axes are task fulfilment, organisation and development, unity/progression/coherence, and language facility. The 0-rule is "merely copies words from the topic" [verbatim]. Label the task in the UI as pre-2023.
