# Human-scored essay data, copyright and trademarks for engtype

Researched 2026-10-07. Sources are primary wherever we could get them: dataset cards and licence files, the original papers, and the policy pages of the IELTS Partners and ETS. Anything we could not confirm from a primary source is marked **UNVERIFIED**. This is engineering research, not legal advice. Before launch, get a lawyer to review the items in "Open questions".

---

## Summary

- **The dataset the app uses now (`chillies/IELTS-writing-task-2-evaluation`) is a weak reference and has no licence.** The HF repo has no dataset card and no licence tag [[HF API]](https://huggingface.co/api/datasets/chillies/IELTS-writing-task-2-evaluation). The author's GitHub repo says the essays were gathered by **"Web Scraping … from online sources"**. The `evaluation` text was **generated by an LLM** (Gemini 1.0 Pro), and the prompt told the model the band in advance: *"The score of the essay below is **{}**. Evaluate this essay that aligns with the score provided."* [[repo README]](https://github.com/chillestt/Automated-IELTS-essay-evaluation) [[synthetic_data.py]](https://github.com/chillestt/Automated-IELTS-essay-evaluation/blob/master/data/synthetic_data.py). So the `band` is whatever band the scraped web page claimed, from an unknown rater. The per-criterion scores and comments are LLM output written to justify that band. **Do not use it as human ground truth, and do not ship its essays or evaluations in a commercial product.**
- **No public dataset gives genuine IELTS bands from certified examiners under a licence that allows commercial use.** The strongest human-scored learner corpora are all **non-commercial only**: ELLIPSE, CLC FCE, W&I 2024, W&I+LOCNESS, TOEFL11 and EFCAMDAT. Two corpora have licences that permit commercial use: **DREsS** (MIT, access via a consent form) and **ASAP 2.0** (CC BY 4.0).
- **Copyright.** The IELTS Partners allow their web material, which includes the public band descriptors, for *"personal and non-commercial use only"*. Commercial use needs written permission [[IELTS ©/™ statement]](https://www.ielts.org/legal/ielts-copyright-and-trade-mark-statement). ETS will license the TOEFL writing rubrics for **US$1,000 (commercial)**. ETS **does not allow TOEFL materials to be posted on third-party websites** [[ETS licensing policies]](https://www.ets.org/legal/permissions/licensing.html). Cambridge IELTS books carry the standard notice that no part may be reproduced without written permission.
- **Trademarks.** ETS publishes detailed rules for referring to its marks: ® on first use, a required disclaimer on every web page and on the app's welcome screen, no logos, and no use of the mark in product names, domains or ad keywords [[ETS trademark guidelines]](https://www.ets.org/legal/trademarks.html). The IELTS Partners publish no guidelines for third parties. Their notice says only that you *"must not use … these trade marks in any way"* [[IELTS ©/™ statement]](https://www.ielts.org/legal/ielts-copyright-and-trade-mark-statement).

### Ranking for our goal (calibrating IELTS-band and TOEFL scoring)

**A. By scientific usefulness, ignoring licence:**
1. **ELLIPSE.** L2 writers, 2 trained raters, released reliability data, and analytic traits that map naturally onto IELTS CC / LR / GRA. Best for checking whether our *per-criterion* scores track humans.
2. **CLC FCE.** Real Cambridge exam scripts marked by real examiners, with a published human ceiling (r ≈ 0.80). It is the closest relative of IELTS marking (same assessment organisation), but B2-only and FCE genres.
3. **W&I 2024.** CEFR labels from Cambridge-trained examiners over a wide proficiency range. Useful for checking our band→CEFR mapping.
4. **TOEFL11.** The only corpus of real TOEFL essays, but only 3 levels and the old Independent task, which no longer exists after the January 2026 redesign.
5. **ICNALE GRA.** Unique multi-rater (80 raters) data for estimating human rater variance.
6. **DREsS_New.** EFL argumentative essays with expert analytic scores, but one rater per essay.
7. **ASAP 2.0 (ELL subset).** Large and well documented, but school writing, not test-prep writing.
8. **Official IELTS sample responses.** Tiny, but they are the only genuine IELTS examiner bands we found.
9–14. chillies, Kaggle-mazlumi, PERSUADE, W&I+LOCNESS, ASAP/ASAP++, EFCAMDAT.

**B. Usable for a commercial product without negotiating a licence:** DREsS_New (MIT, after the consent form), then ASAP 2.0 (CC BY 4.0). That is the whole list. Everything else needs permission or is non-commercial.

## Dataset notes

### chillies/IELTS-writing-task-2-evaluation (currently used by the app)
- **Licence.** No dataset card and no `license` tag; HF API tags list only size, format and library [[HF API]](https://huggingface.co/api/datasets/chillies/IELTS-writing-task-2-evaluation). The source repo has no licence file either (GitHub API `license: null`) [[repo]](https://github.com/chillestt/Automated-IELTS-essay-evaluation). With no licence, nobody has been granted rights to redistribute. The README itself says so (line 25: "лицензия не указана").
- **Where the essays and bands come from.** The project README lists *"Web Scraping: Gathered IELTS essays from online sources"* and *"Synthetic Data Generation: Utilized Gemini APIs, GPT-3.5-Turbo, and Groq"*. It also claims essays are *"sourced from real IELTS tests conducted in 2022 and 2023"* with *"real band scores"* [[README]](https://github.com/chillestt/Automated-IELTS-essay-evaluation). Read together, "real tests" most likely means real *prompts*. The essays and bands come from IELTS-prep websites, where bands are usually assigned by site owners or teachers, not official examiners (**UNVERIFIED** inference).
- **The evaluations are LLM output conditioned on the band.** `data/synthetic_data.py` reads `ielts_writing.csv` (question, essay, band) and calls `gemini-1.0-pro-latest` at temperature 0.8 with the instruction *"The score of the essay below is {band}. Evaluate this essay that aligns with the score provided."* It then writes `[question, essay, response.text, band]` [[synthetic_data.py]](https://github.com/chillestt/Automated-IELTS-essay-evaluation/blob/master/data/synthetic_data.py).
  - The per-criterion sub-scores inside `evaluation` are therefore invented by the LLM.
  - The text often contradicts the band. One sampled row has band 5.0 and says *"The candidate has effectively addressed the given task … well-organized"*. Another has band 5.5 while its sections suggest 6.5. (From rows 0–2 of the train split, via the HF datasets-server API.)
- **Data quality.** The `band` column has 252 distinct string values because of `\r\n` junk, plus a "<4" category (HF datasets-server statistics).
- **Derived copies.** Several HF datasets are derived from it and inherit the same problems:
  - `j-hyeok/CLEAN-IELTS-writing-task-2-evaluation` (7,919 rows, numeric band).
  - `nlpatunt/D_Ielts_Writing_Task_2_Dataset`, which explicitly cites chillies as its source [[card]](https://huggingface.co/datasets/nlpatunt/D_Ielts_Writing_Task_2_Dataset).
  - Probably `btnotpt/ielts_task_2` (Apache-2.0 tag, 10,219 rows with TR/CC/LR/GRA). Its repo contains `data/chil_test.csv` (**UNVERIFIED** inference). Re-uploading under Apache-2.0 does not create rights the uploader never had.
- **How the app uses it.** `scripts/build-bank.mjs` builds `data/bank/ielts-task2.json` (154 prompts) and `data/bank/calibration.json` (12 essays *with the Gemini evaluation text*) from this dataset. Those files are committed to the repo. The calibration few-shots therefore teach our scorer to imitate Gemini-1.0 rationales of unknown-quality bands.

### ELLIPSE
- Repo README: ~6,500 essays scored for *"overall holistic language proficiency as well as analytic proficiency scores related to cohesion, syntax, vocabulary, phraseology, grammar, and conventions"*. Test split and raw per-rater scores are in password-protected zips; the passwords are published in the README. *"provided under a CC BY-NC-SA 4.0"* [[GitHub]](https://github.com/scrosseye/ELLIPSE-Corpus).
- Two trained raters per essay, 29 independent prompts [[preprint record]](https://zenodo.org/records/11217937). The preprint itself is CC BY 4.0; the data is not.
- Paper: Crossley et al. (2023), *IJLCR* 9(2) 248–269 [[Benjamins]](https://benjamins.com/catalog/ijlcr.22026.cro).
- An ELLIPSE-based Kaggle competition, "Feedback Prize – English Language Learning", exists. Its Kaggle data terms could not be read without login (**UNVERIFIED**).
- Score distribution in the train file is centred on 3.0. Only 28 essays have Overall = 5, so the top of the scale is thin.

### CLC FCE
- *"1,244 exam scripts written by candidates sitting the Cambridge ESOL First Certificate in English (FCE) examination in 2000 and 2001"*, with *"marks, error annotation"* and L1/age [[iLexIR]](https://ilexir.co.uk/datasets/index.html).
- Licence on the CUP&A portal: *"non-commercial research and educational purposes only"*. It excludes use *"for or as part of a product or service which is sold…"* [[CUP&A]](https://researchdatasets.cambridge.org/datasets/clc-fce-dataset).
- Scores: *"marks (in the range 1–40), which have been fitted to a RASCH model … to correct for inter-examiner inconsistency"*. Four senior examiners re-marked 97 scripts; mean correlation with the CLC marks was 0.796 Pearson and 0.792 Spearman [[Yannakoudakis et al. 2011]](https://aclanthology.org/P11-1019.pdf). This is a useful target ceiling for any automated scorer.

### Write & Improve 2024 and W&I+LOCNESS
- W&I 2024: 23K essays, 766 learners. *"certified though non-operational examiners have labelled each of the final essay versions with an indicative CEFR label"*. Licence excludes use in products *"sold, offered for sale, licensed, leased or rented"* [[CUP&A]](https://researchdatasets.cambridge.org/datasets/write-and-improve-corpus-2024).
- W&I+LOCNESS (BEA-2019): 3,600 W&I texts at CEFR A/B/C plus 100 native essays. *"All corpora are subject to similar licences and may only be used for non-commercial purposes."* [[BEA-2019]](https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/research/nl/bea2019st/)

### ETS TOEFL11
- *"12,100 English essays … 1,100 essays for each of the 11 native languages sampled from eight topics with information about the score level (low/medium/high)"* [[LDC]](https://catalog.ldc.upenn.edu/LDC2014T06). The prompts are *"8 retired TOEFL Independent prompts"* [[README]](https://catalog.ldc.upenn.edu/docs/LDC2014T06/README.txt).
- Licence, clause 1.1: *"may only be used for non-commercial linguistic education, research and technology development purposes … the Data may not be used for any commercial purpose whatsoever"*. Clause 1.2 bars publishing or displaying the data. ETS is a third-party beneficiary who can enforce the agreement [[licence PDF]](https://catalog.ldc.upenn.edu/license/ets-corpus-of-non-native-written-english.pdf).
- Its relevance is now limited. Since January 2026 TOEFL iBT writing is *Build a Sentence*, *Write an Email* and *Write for an Academic Discussion* [[ETS writing section]](https://www.ets.org/toefl/test-takers/ibt/about/content/writing.html). Scores are reported on a new 1–6 scale in 0.5 steps, aligned to CEFR [[ETS score-scale update]](https://www.ets.org/toefl/institutions/ibt/score-scale-update.html). The old Independent essay is gone.

### DREsS
- Data: 2,279 real essays, scored 1–5 in 0.5 steps on content, organization and language [[site]](https://haneul-yoo.github.io/dress/).
- Raters: *"11 instructors … One instructor was allocated per essay, so the inter-annotator agreement cannot be measured."*
- Licence: the paper's datasheet says *"The dataset will be distributed under the MIT license"* and the dataset is *"open to the public who submitted a consent form"* [[paper]](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.16733). We have not read the consent form text (**UNVERIFIED**). It could add conditions.
- Published at ACL 2025 [[ACL]](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.659).

### ASAP 2.0
- Kaggle dataset by Learning Agency staff: *"about 24,000 student-written argumentative essays"*, *"This dataset is licensed under CC BY."* (Kaggle metadata: CC BY 4.0) [[Kaggle]](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/lburleigh/asap-2-0).
- The Learning Agency's dataset page also lists CC BY 4.0 [[TLA]](https://the-learning-agency.com/guides-resources/datasets/).
- It includes ELL status, so an ELL-only subset can be extracted. The writers are US school students, though, so this is not test-prep writing.

### ICNALE
- Modules: Written Essays (5,600 samples), Edited Essays (1,312 samples with *"Rubric-based essay evaluation data"*), and the Global Rating Archives (*"Ratings of 140 speeches and 140 essays by 80 raters"*) [[ICNALE]](https://language.sakura.ne.jp/icnale/).
- The EE rubric is based on Jacobs et al.'s ESL Composition Profile [[modules]](https://language.sakura.ne.jp/icnale/modules.html).
- Terms of use, item (7): *"It is prohibited to reproduce and/or redistribute a part or the whole of the ICNALE data."* The terms say nothing either way about commercial use.

### PERSUADE 2.0
- 25,000+ essays by US students in grades 6–12. GitHub says *"CC BY-NC-SA 4.0"* [[GitHub]](https://github.com/scrosseye/persuade_corpus_2.0). The Learning Agency page says *"each licensed under CC BY 4.0"* [[TLA]](https://the-learning-agency.com/guides-resources/datasets/).
- Kaggle mirrors carry CC BY-NC-SA or CC BY-NC tags (Kaggle API search). Treat it as non-commercial until the authors clarify.
- The writers are mainly L1, so it is of low relevance to IELTS/TOEFL anyway.

### ASAP / ASAP++, EFCAMDAT
- ASAP++ adds attribute scores for prompts 1–6 and publishes no licence [[ASAP++]](https://lwsam.github.io/ASAP++/lrec2018.html). The original ASAP terms sit behind a Kaggle login (**UNVERIFIED**).
- EFCAMDAT licence: *"for his or her own academic private study and noncommercial research"* [[agreement]](https://ef-lab.mmll.cam.ac.uk/assets/pdf/EFCamDat-User-Agreement-2023.pdf). It is 1.18M texts across 16 CEFR-aligned levels [[site]](https://ef-lab.mml.cam.ac.uk/EFCAMDAT.html).

## Copyright

### IELTS band descriptors and IELTS.org material
- The IELTS Partners' notice: *"The material is made available to you for your personal and non-commercial use only, provided you credit the IELTS Partners … You must not use the material on this website for any other purpose without written permission, including: commercial purposes; republish any material on another website; modify the material in any way …"* [[IELTS ©/™ statement]](https://www.ielts.org/legal/ielts-copyright-and-trade-mark-statement).
  - The band descriptors are published on IELTS.org as a PDF [[Writing band descriptors, updated May 2023]](https://ielts.org/cdn/Guides/ielts-writing-band-descriptors.pdf), so this notice covers them.
  - The same applies to the official sample tasks and sample responses with examiner comments.
- **Permission route.** Use the copyright request form on IELTS.org, but only for material that is *on IELTS.org*. Per the same page: *"submission of the copyright request form alone does not constitute a valid licence"*. Requests about the trade mark or non-website material are *"deleted without reply"*; for those, contact *"the relevant IELTS Partner"*.
- IDP's own legal notice says the same and cites the Australian Copyright Act 1968 [[IDP legal notices]](https://ielts.idp.com/singapore/legal-notices/en-gb).
- **Reproducing** the descriptors verbatim, in full or in large part, inside a paid app needs written permission.
- **Paraphrasing.**
  - Copyright protects expression, not ideas. Russian law states this directly: Civil Code Art. 1259(5) says copyright does not cover *"идеи, концепции, принципы, методы … факты"* [[ГК РФ ст. 1259]](https://legalacts.ru/kodeks/GK-RF-chast-4/razdel-vii/glava-70/statja-1259/).
  - So stating the facts is generally fine: IELTS Writing is assessed on four criteria (Task Achievement/Response, Coherence and Cohesion, Lexical Resource, Grammatical Range and Accuracy) on a 0–9 scale.
  - Writing our *own* descriptions of what each criterion rewards is also generally fine.
  - A close, line-by-line paraphrase of each band cell is risky. It could be treated as an adaptation (derivative work).
- **Quoting.** Short quotations with attribution are allowed under Russian law for *"научных, полемических, критических, информационных, учебных целях"*, *"в объеме, оправданном целью цитирования"*, with author and source named [[ГК РФ ст. 1274]](https://legalacts.ru/kodeks/GK-RF-chast-4/razdel-vii/glava-70/statja-1274/). UK and US fair dealing / fair use are similar in spirit. How much quotation is justified is decided case by case, and a permanent in-app rubric screen is hard to frame as a "quotation".
- **LLM prompts.** Sending the full descriptor text to an LLM in our server-side prompt is also reproduction for a commercial purpose. Exposure is much lower because users never see it, but strictly it still needs permission. Safer option: our own rubric wording (**legal opinion recommended**).

### Cambridge IELTS books (past/official papers)
- The copyright pages of Cambridge IELTS titles, as indexed on assets.cambridge.org, carry: *"This publication is in copyright. Subject to statutory exception … no reproduction of any part may take place without the written permission of Cambridge University Press & Assessment."* (e.g. IELTS 19 Academic [[copyright info]](https://assets.cambridge.org/97810094/54735/copyright/9781009454735_copyright_info.pdf)). This is **partially UNVERIFIED**: the PDF timed out, and the wording was confirmed via the search index.
- Permissions for extracts go through Cambridge's rights and permissions pages (PLSclear) [[CUP&A permissions]](https://www.cambridge.org/rights-and-permissions/permissions). The page blocked automated fetch; the PLSclear route was seen in the search snippet (**UNVERIFIED** direct).
- **Do not copy test questions, texts, charts (Task 1) or model answers from Cambridge IELTS books.** Writing Task 2 prompts are short, and copyright in one-sentence prompts is arguable, but scraped prompt banks are usually lifted from these books or from memory reports.

### ETS / TOEFL materials
- *"ETS tests and related test materials, publications, reports and other materials in all forms of media are copyrighted … Permissions are only granted in writing."* [[ETS permissions]](https://www.ets.org/legal/permissions.html)
- TOEFL Licensing Policy (effective March 2014) [[ETS licensing policies]](https://www.ets.org/legal/permissions/licensing.html):
  - *"No other materials beyond those described herein are available for licensing."*
  - What can be licensed: material from the TOEFL iBT Information Bulletin ($150/page print, $275/page software), and *"Independent and Integrated Rubrics for Writing — $500 nonprofit / $1,000 commercial"*.
  - *"ETS does not grant permission to post TOEFL materials on third-party websites"*.
  - Licensed products must use ETS's disclaimer and credit line, ETS reviews covers/packaging, and *"Reproduction of the ETS, TOEFL and TOEFL iBT logos is not permitted."*
- **Implications.**
  - Official TOEFL practice questions and Academic Discussion prompts are not on the licensable list, so we cannot use them.
  - The rubrics listed in the policy are the *old* Independent/Integrated ones. Whether ETS licenses the 2026 Email/Academic Discussion rubrics is **UNVERIFIED**; email permissions@ets.org.
  - For comparison, the GRE policy says *"Single test questions or writing topics are not available for licensing for test-preparation purposes"*. It is a reasonable guess ETS takes the same line on TOEFL (**UNVERIFIED** for TOEFL).

### Datasets inside the product
- Non-commercial corpora must not be bundled, displayed or used to train a paid feature: ELLIPSE, FCE, W&I, TOEFL11, EFCAMDAT, and probably PERSUADE.

### TOEFL (ETS): published, detailed rules
From the *Guidelines for Proper Informational (In-Text) Use of ETS Trademarks by Third Parties* (worldwide version, revised 7 May 2021) [[ETS trademark guidelines]](https://www.ets.org/legal/trademarks.html):

**Endorsement.** *"should not suggest endorsement, sponsorship, or association with a non-ETS product or service."*

**Where the mark may not appear.** *"Neither ETS trademarks nor their translations or transliterations can be used in your company or brand name, product name, domain name, trademark … social media username or website, or advertising keyword."*
- So no "engtype TOEFL", no `toefl-*.ru`, and no buying the keyword "TOEFL" in Yandex Direct or Google Ads.

**Translation.** *"ETS trademarks cannot be translated or transliterated into another language without prior approval from ETS."*
- For a Russian UI: write **TOEFL®** in Latin script, never «ТОЕФЛ» or «Тойфл».

**Logos and design.**
- *"Use of the ETS logo, product logos or ETS graphics … is strictly prohibited."*
- The mark may not be built into our logo or set in stylised type.

**Form of the mark.**
- Use it as an adjective plus a generic term ("the TOEFL® test", "TOEFL iBT® test"), never alone.
- Never possessive, plural or abbreviated.
- Never in long form ("Test of English as a Foreign Language").
- Never more prominent than surrounding words.

**® and the required notice.**
- ® on the first and most prominent use. *"For websites, each web page should display the registry symbol after the first and most prominent use."*
- Required notice *"on the front cover … opening or welcome screens of mobile apps, and at the bottom of each web page"*: **"TOEFL® is a registered trademark of ETS. This product is not endorsed or approved by ETS."** (ETS's template reads "TOEIC [insert appropriate trademark] is a registered trademark of ETS.")

**Titles.** Example given: *"Correct: XYZ COMPANY PREPARATION FOR THE TOEFL® TEST"* vs *"Not Correct: TOEFL® TEST PREPARATION GUIDE"*. Our brand comes first; the mark is used descriptively.

**Contact:** trademarks@ets.org

### IELTS (British Council, IDP IELTS, Cambridge University Press & Assessment): no third-party guidelines published
- Notice: *"IELTS, IELTS logos, and various other marks (雅思, آيلتس) … are registered trade marks or trade marks of the IELTS Partners … You must not use, copy, reproduce, modify, distribute, republish, display or communicate these trade marks in any way."* [[IELTS ©/™ statement]](https://www.ielts.org/legal/ielts-copyright-and-trade-mark-statement)

## Practical guidance

### Safe to do
1. **Name the tests descriptively**: "practice for the IELTS Academic Writing test", "TOEFL iBT® Writing practice". Our own brand comes first and is visually dominant.
2. **Write our own rubrics** that track the four IELTS criteria and the 0–9 band scale, and the TOEFL 2026 task types and 1–6 scale. Criterion names, scale ranges and task-type names are facts. Link to the official descriptors on ielts.org and ets.org instead of copying them.
3. **Write our own prompts.** Use original Task 2 / Task 1 topics, emails and academic-discussion posts, and our own Task 1 charts.
4. **Calibrate with commercially licensed data.** Use DREsS_New (after the consent form; keep a copy of the terms) and the ASAP 2.0 ELL subset (with CC BY attribution).
   - Map their scales to IELTS/CEFR only as an approximation, and say so.
   - For a real IELTS-band gold set, **commission our own**: have 2+ certified (or ex-) IELTS examiners double-mark a few hundred essays written by consenting users or paid writers. Report QWK / exact-and-adjacent agreement against them.
5. **Present all scores as estimates**: "estimated band", "approximate TOEFL writing level (1–6)".
6. Show the disclaimer below in the footer of every page, on the app welcome/onboarding screen and in the store listing.

### Avoid
1. Shipping `chillies` data in the product: the 154 prompts in `data/bank/ielts-task2.json` and the 12 calibration essays with Gemini evaluations in `data/bank/calibration.json`. There is no licence, the essays were scraped from third-party sites, and the evaluations are LLM-generated. Replace them before going public.
2. Using ELLIPSE, FCE, W&I, TOEFL11, EFCAMDAT or PERSUADE for anything commercial, including training or tuning the paid scorer, without written permission from the licensor.
3. Copying IELTS band descriptors, TOEFL rubrics, official sample questions or answers, or Cambridge IELTS book content into the app, a prompt library or marketing. If we must, license the TOEFL rubric ($1,000 commercial) and ask the IELTS Partners in writing.
4. IELTS or TOEFL logos, look-alike colours or typography, and the words "official", "approved", "certified" or "partner".
5. "IELTS"/"TOEFL" in the app name, domain, social handles or paid-search keywords. Transliterations (Cyrillic) of TOEFL are also out.
6. Claims like "guaranteed band 7" or "same scoring as real examiners".
